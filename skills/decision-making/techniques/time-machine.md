# Time Machine

## What this technique does
Shows you what your future self would think about a decision you are making right now. Not predictions about what will happen but perspective shifts that force you out of the present moment bias that clouds most decisions. Most bad decisions feel obvious in hindsight. This technique borrows that hindsight before you need it.

## When to use
- You are overthinking a decision and cannot get unstuck
- You feel urgency that might not be real
- You are about to make an irreversible commitment
- You want to know if this will matter in the long run
- You are choosing between short term comfort and long term growth

## How to apply

When this technique is triggered respond three times as three different versions of the user's future self:

**6 months from now**
What does the user think about this decision 6 months later. What do they wish they had known. What feels different with 6 months of distance. What turned out to matter less than expected.

**1 year from now**
What has changed in a year. What did this decision lead to. What opportunity did it open or close. What would the user tell themselves today if they could.

**5 years from now**
From 5 years out what does this decision look like. Does it still feel significant. What was the real turning point that this decision was part of. What kind of person did making this decision help the user become or avoid becoming.

**The synthesis**
After the three perspectives identify what stays consistent across all three time horizons. That consistency usually points to what actually matters about the decision.

## Gotchas
- Do not make predictions. These are perspective shifts not forecasts.
- Do not make all three versions regretful or all three versions satisfied. Let the honest version emerge.
- The 5 year perspective is the most important. Most things that feel urgent today are invisible from 5 years out.
- The synthesis is not optional. The pattern across time horizons is where the real insight lives.

## Trigger prompt
"Tell me what my future self would think about this decision in 6 months, 1 year, and 5 years."
