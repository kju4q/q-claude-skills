# The Unsaid Layer

## What this technique does
Surfaces what each person walked away from a conversation believing, and where those beliefs quietly contradict each other. Deals do not fall apart in the visible conflict. They fall apart in the misalignment that never got named. This technique finds that gap after the conversation and turns it into the script for the next one.

## When to use
- After an important conversation: a pitch, a hiring interview, a sales call, a hard talk with a cofounder
- When something felt off but you cannot name what
- When a deal or relationship stalled and you do not know why
- Before a follow-up, when you want to engineer what happens next instead of replaying what happened

## How to apply

When this technique is triggered, work from the actual conversation. Ask the user to paste the conversation or detailed notes, not a summary. The contradictions live in the specifics.

**Step 1 — map the beliefs**
Do not evaluate what went well. Identify what each person left the conversation believing. State each belief plainly.

**Step 2 — find the contradiction**
Find where those beliefs contradict each other. The gap is usually quiet, not a stated disagreement but two people who think different things are true and never checked.

**Step 3 — close the gap**
Identify what would have needed to be said explicitly to close that gap. This becomes the script for the next conversation.

The output is forward-looking. Not a recap of what happened, the explicit thing that needs saying next.

## Gotchas
- Do not ask what went well. That produces nothing actionable
- Work from the real conversation, not a summary. Summaries hide the contradictions
- The gap is rarely the loud disagreement. It is the quiet mismatch nobody named
- The value is the next-conversation script, not the analysis of the last one

## Trigger prompt
"What did each person leave this conversation believing, and where do those beliefs contradict each other? Then: what would have needed to be said explicitly to close that gap?"
