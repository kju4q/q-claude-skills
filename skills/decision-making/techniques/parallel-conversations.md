# Parallel Conversations

## What this technique does
Runs the same idea through three completely different perspectives simultaneously. Most people have one conversation and think they have the full picture. They have one third of it. The supporter shows you the opportunity. The skeptic shows you the risk. The neutral shows you reality.

## When to use
- You are too close to an idea to see it clearly
- You need to make a decision but feel attached to one outcome
- You want to pressure test something before committing to it
- You need to present an idea to different stakeholders with different incentives

## How to apply

Open three separate conversations. Give each one the same idea but a completely different role:

**Conversation 1 — The Supporter**
"Build the strongest possible case for why this will work. What is the opportunity. Why is the timing right. Why will people want this."

**Conversation 2 — The Skeptic**
"Destroy this idea. Why will it fail. What assumption am I making that is probably wrong. What does someone who strongly disagrees say."

**Conversation 3 — The Neutral**
"No encouragement, no discouragement. Just facts and logic. What does the landscape look like for this. What has worked and what has not worked for similar things."

Then read all three and synthesize. The insight lives in the contrast between them not in any single output.

## Gotchas
- Do not run all three in one conversation. They influence each other and the outputs lose their independence.
- Do not skip the neutral. Most people only want support or critique. The neutral is usually the most useful.
- Synthesize after reading all three. The insight lives in the contrast between them not in any single output.
- Use Claude Projects to make this faster — set up each project with the role pre-loaded so you never have to re-explain.

## Trigger prompt
"I want you to be the most skeptical version of this idea. Destroy it."
