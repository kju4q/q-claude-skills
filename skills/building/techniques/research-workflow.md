# Research Workflow

## What this technique does
Turns a research question into a structured workflow. Most people start researching by searching randomly and stop when they feel like they know enough. This technique gives research a clear structure — what to look for, where to look, how to synthesize what you find, and how to know when you actually have enough to move forward.

## When to use
- You are entering a new space and need to get up to speed fast
- You have a decision to make that requires outside information
- You feel like you are going in circles with your research
- You want to produce a clear output from your research not just accumulate information

## How to apply

When this technique is triggered do not start searching immediately. Build the research structure first.

**Step 1 — Define the research question**
What exactly are you trying to find out. A good research question is specific enough that you would recognize the answer if you found it. Broad questions produce broad research that never ends.

**Step 2 — Map what you already know**
Before looking for new information identify what you already know about this topic. What assumptions are you starting with. What would confirm them. What would challenge them.

**Step 3 — Identify the sources**
Where does the best information on this topic actually live. Primary sources, expert voices, data sets, case studies. Rank them by reliability before you start.

**Step 4 — Define the synthesis structure**
Before researching decide what format the output will take. A decision, a summary, a comparison, a recommendation. Knowing the output format shapes what information is actually useful to collect.

**Step 5 — Set the stopping condition**
How will you know when you have enough. Define this before you start or you will research forever. A good stopping condition is: I have enough to make this decision with confidence or I have found three independent sources that agree on this point.

## Gotchas
- Do not start with Google. Start with the best source for this specific question.
- Accumulating information is not research. Research ends with a clear output.
- The stopping condition is the most important step. Without it research becomes procrastination.
- What you already know shapes what you find. Map your assumptions first or your research will confirm them without you realizing it.

## Trigger prompt
"Help me build a research workflow for this question. Cover what to look for, where to look, and how to know when I have enough."
