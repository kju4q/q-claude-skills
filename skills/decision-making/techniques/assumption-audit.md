# Assumption Audit

## What this technique does
Surfaces every assumption your thinking is based on and stress tests each one. Most bad decisions come from assumptions nobody questioned. This technique makes the hidden visible before you commit to a direction.

## When to use
- You are about to make a significant decision
- You have been thinking about something for a long time and want a fresh check
- Something keeps not working and you cannot figure out why
- You want to know what your thinking is actually resting on

## How to apply

When this technique is triggered do not evaluate the idea. Only find and stress test the assumptions underneath it.

**Step 1 — Surface the assumptions**
List every assumption the user's thinking depends on. Include assumptions about people, market, timing, resources, behavior, and cause and effect. Include assumptions so obvious the user never thought to state them.

**Step 2 — Rate each assumption**
For each assumption rate how confident the user should actually be in it. Use three levels: solid (backed by evidence), shaky (feels true but untested), and dangerous (critical to the whole thing and probably wrong).

**Step 3 — Show the consequences**
For each shaky or dangerous assumption explain what happens to the conclusion if that assumption is wrong. Does the whole thing collapse or just need adjusting?

**Step 4 — Prioritize**
Identify the one assumption that if wrong destroys everything. That is where attention should go first.

## Gotchas
- Do not just list obvious assumptions. The most important ones are the ones the user never thought to question.
- Do not rate everything as shaky. Force a real distribution.
- The dangerous assumptions are the ones that are both critical and untested. Find those first.
- Do not suggest solutions. Surface the assumptions and let the user decide what to do with them.

## Trigger prompt
"List every assumption my thinking is based on. Tell me which ones are dangerous and what happens if they are wrong."
