# The Constraint That Becomes the Strategy

## What this technique does
Turns your biggest limitation into a competitive position. Most people try to overcome or hide their constraints. This technique uses the constraint as a filter that reveals moves a well-resourced competitor would never make, and finds the ones they could not copy without breaking what already works for them.

## When to use
- You are frustrated by a limitation: budget, team size, market timing, no network
- You are trying to find a differentiated position against bigger competitors
- You keep thinking about your strengths and it is not producing an edge
- You want to turn a weakness into a moat

## How to apply

When this technique is triggered, take the user's biggest current limitation and run two passes.

**Pass 1 — the constraint as forcing function**
Ask: what does this constraint force me to do that a well-resourced competitor would never bother doing? Surface the moves the constraint pushes the user into that better-resourced players skip because they do not have to.

**Pass 2 — the unreplicable filter**
Ask: which of those things, if done exceptionally well, would be impossible for someone with more resources to replicate without breaking something that already works for them? This is the real competitive position. Not just different, structurally hard for a bigger player to copy.

Be specific. Vague constraints ("small budget") produce vague output. Concrete constraints ("$2k, no team, 10 hours a week") produce sharp output.

## Gotchas
- Do not let the user frame the constraint as something to overcome. The whole point is to use it
- Pass 1 alone is not enough. The value is in Pass 2, the unreplicable filter
- Generic strengths analysis defeats the technique. Stay anchored to the specific limitation
- The best answers are moves a competitor would actively avoid, not just neglect

## Trigger prompt
"Here is my biggest limitation: [constraint]. What does it force me to do that a well-resourced competitor would never bother doing? Then: which of those would be impossible for them to replicate without breaking something that already works for them?"
