# Weekly Planning

## What this technique does
Builds a complete weekly plan from your actual context — calendar, notes, health goals, and work priorities. Not generic time blocks but specific actions tied to your real life. The plan is only as good as the context you give it so this technique is designed around loading everything Claude needs before asking for anything.

## When to use
- You are starting a new week and want a clear plan
- You feel scattered and need to see everything in one place
- You want to connect your health goals to your work schedule
- You need specific actions not vague intentions

## How to apply

When this technique is triggered do not generate a generic weekly plan. Build one from the user's actual context.

**Step 1 — Load the context**
Before planning anything gather:
- Calendar commitments for the week
- Open tasks and projects
- Health goals and constraints
- Energy patterns and best working hours
- What did not get done last week and why

**Step 2 — Identify the priorities**
From the context identify the three things that if done this week would make it a successful week. Everything else organizes around these.

**Step 3 — Build the plan**
Assign specific actions to specific days and times. Include:
- Work blocks tied to actual projects not vague categories
- Health and recovery built in not bolted on
- Buffer time for the unexpected
- Preparation time before important events

**Step 4 — The constraint check**
Before finalizing ask: what is most likely to derail this plan. Build in the mitigation before the week starts not after it goes wrong.

## Gotchas
- Generic time blocks are not a plan. Every block needs a specific output.
- Health is not optional. A plan that ignores recovery will not survive contact with reality.
- Buffer time is not wasted time. A plan with no slack is a plan that fails by Wednesday.
- The constraint check is what separates a real plan from an optimistic fantasy.

## Trigger prompt
"Build me a complete weekly plan based on everything I have just told you about my life and work. Be specific. No generic blocks."
