# The Five Chains

Each chain is four messages and the part you need is never in the first, because a first answer is the most likely helpful response averaged across everyone who ever asked something similar, which makes it agreeable by design.

**The one rule:** run all four messages in the same chat, one after another, and never start a new conversation between steps. Each message has to react to the answer before it, and that is the whole reason the fourth answer comes out different from the first. Split them across separate chats and you have five ordinary questions.

Replace anything in brackets with your own.

## Which one to reach for

| You are | Use |
|---|---|
| Committed to something and want it stress tested | The Devil's Advocate |
| Unable to tell how your work reads to other people | The Persona Chain |
| Struggling to explain it simply | The Compression Chain |
| Uneasy about a plan you cannot fault | The Assumption Killer |
| Unsure whether you are early, late, or on time | The Time Machine |

---

## 1. The Devil's Advocate

**Message 1**
```
My idea is [describe it in a few sentences: what it is, who it is for,
and how it makes money if it does].
```

**Message 2**
```
Destroy it. Do not hedge, do not offer balance, and do not tell me what
is good about it. Name the one assumption that everything else depends
on, then the three most likely ways this does not work out. Give me the
actual sequence of events, not a category of risk.
```

**Message 3**
```
Now rebuild it, using your own criticisms as the design constraints.
What is the version of this that survives everything you just said?
```

**Message 4**
```
Now write it as if it already worked. Two years out, it succeeded. What
did it end up being, and which of the changes you just made was the one
that actually mattered?
```

That last question is the one people skip, and it is the one that tells you what to do on Monday.

## 2. The Persona Chain

**Message 1**
```
Here is something I have written: [paste it].
```

**Message 2**
```
Rewrite this exactly as a skeptical investor would read it, including
what they would think and not say out loud.
```

**Message 3**
```
Now rewrite it as an excited customer would read it. What are they
picturing that I never actually promised?
```

**Message 4**
```
Now as a competitor who wants to copy this. What would they take, what
would they skip, and how long would it take them?
```

Message three is the quiet one, because the gap between what people imagine and what you actually said is where refunds and bad reviews come from.

---

## 3. The Compression Chain

**Message 1**
```
Here is how I explain what I am building right now: [paste your full
explanation, however long it is].
```

**Message 2**
```
Compress that to five sentences. Keep what matters and cut what I am
attached to.
```

**Message 3**
```
Now one sentence.
```

**Message 4**
```
Now three words.
```

Watch what falls out between five sentences and three words, because whatever survives is what you are actually building and whatever disappears was decoration you were defending.

---

## 4. The Assumption Killer

**Message 1**
```
Here is my plan: [what you are going to do, in order, including how you
expect people to find it and why you think they will pay].
```

**Message 2**
```
List every assumption I am making. Include the ones I am not aware I am
making, and the ones so obvious they did not seem worth stating.
```

**Message 3**
```
Which one of those is most likely to be wrong, and why that one
specifically?
```

**Message 4**
```
Rewrite the plan assuming that assumption is false. Do not soften it,
just assume it is simply not true.
```

The rewrite in message four is usually a better plan than the one you started with, whether or not the assumption turns out to be wrong.

---

## 5. The Time Machine

**Message 1**
```
My project is [describe it].
```

**Message 2**
```
How would someone in 2019 react to this? What would they not believe?
```

**Message 3**
```
Now someone in 2030, to whom this is completely obvious. What do they
think took so long?
```

**Message 4**
```
Now a historian in 2050, writing about what I got wrong about this
moment. Be specific about the mistake.
```

Adjust the years to sit roughly seven back, four forward, and far enough out that nobody alive is defending it.

## Going deeper

Four of these exist in this repo as full techniques, with when to use them, how to apply them, and the gotchas that make the difference between running one properly and going through the motions.

- [Adversarial Room](../skills/research/techniques/adversarial-room.md) is the Devil's Advocate taken further, into the fatal assumption and what a competitor who understood it would do
- [Assumption Audit](../skills/decision-making/techniques/assumption-audit.md) is the Assumption Killer with a rating system, so you can tell a shaky assumption from a dangerous one
- [Parallel Conversations](../skills/decision-making/techniques/parallel-conversations.md) runs a supporter, a skeptic, and a neutral in three separate chats, which is the same idea as a chain turned sideways
- [Time Machine](../skills/decision-making/techniques/time-machine.md) is the version for personal decisions, at six months, one year, and five years, rather than the version above for project timing

If you want these to run without you pasting anything, they are installable as skills. Instructions are in the [README](../README.md).

## What these cannot do

None of them have met your customer. What comes back is not a verdict, it is a set of claims specific enough that you can go and check them, and the checking is still yours to do. A chain that produces three things worth verifying has done its job, and a chain that produces a conclusion you accept without checking has quietly done the opposite.

---

None of these tell you whether your idea is good. They tell you what you have been avoiding looking at.
